# Town Office Spending as % of Municipal Budget — Most Recent Available Budget

Middlesex spends 5.74% of its municipal budget on office staff (Clerk, Treasurer, Assistants) — ranking #9 of 18 comparable Vermont towns. That is the median. With the Town Administrator included, it rises to 8.95% and rank #4, but still within the range of towns that have made the same hire.

Middlesex is taking on more roadwork — maintaining culverts to avoid costlier repairs, picking up more town-managed Class 3 roads — and the administrative burden and grant opportunities grow with it. The data shows that towns at this point add a Town Administrator, in a majority of towns, to make sure this work gets done right.

Rather than adding more Assistant Town Clerk hours, towns with a similar budget and population often have only a part-time Treasurer alongside a full-time Clerk. Middlesex's move this year to a full-time Clerk, full-time Treasurer, and fewer assistant hours is consistent with how these other towns staff their offices.

### What about the tax rate?

Middlesex's municipal tax rate, adjusted by Common Level of Appraisal (CLA), is around the 75th percentile statewide — roughly 60 towns pay more, 200 pay less. That does not mean Middlesex spends more than average. Middlesex spends an average amount but has almost no commercial tax base. Towns with ski areas, retail corridors, or industrial parks spread the same costs across more taxpayers and end up with lower residential rates.

The bottom line: Middlesex cannot meaningfully cut its budget without either reducing roadwork — putting roads at risk after back-to-back flood years — or cutting office staff and risking the town's ability to meet its statutory requirements — and legal costs that exceed whatever was saved.
